Collection Receipt Proposal

Drag-and-drop reconciliation board for collection sheets in Proposal.

The collection addon already models what a cobranza needs, but not the step that distributes the money: an operator had to edit amount_applied row by row with no view of the whole sheet. This addon adds that step — the phase between Confirm and Close.

A board opens from a collection.sheet in proposal (header button Receipt Proposal) and reads left to right in two columns. The first holds the unassigned pool: vouchers with money still to distribute, which by definition answer to no customer debt yet. The second stacks one card per collection.allocation, and inside each of them one card per circuit (document_kind invoice / quotation) carrying that circuit’s figures and the collection.receipt.value rows applied to it. Ver detalle, on the circuit card’s header line, expands that card downwards in place — the card keeps its width, so the customer under the pointer does not re-lay out — to show that debt in full: its documents, their balances and the editable imputation. As many circuits stay expanded as the operator wants, and Escape closes them all.

A voucher is dragged from the pool onto the FAC or PRE card of a customer, so the drop itself answers which circuit the money belongs to; it may be split across customers of the same sheet.

A real planilla carries dozens of customers and a pool of mixed payment methods, so one wrapping line of controls sits above the board — paid once, never once per customer. It searches the lane by customer name or CUIT (accent-blind: nunez finds Núñez), narrows every customer card to one circuit, narrows the pool to one payment method, and reorders the lane by customer, by debt, by collected, or by what is collected and still unimputed — the sort that puts the work that is left at the top. Every one of them reads a figure the board already has, so nothing is asked of the server and no filter outlives the screen. Filters hide and never disable: what they take away, the same control puts back. The figures follow what is shown and name the sheet’s own beside them, so a filtered header never passes itself off as the whole planilla — except the pool’s Requiere asignación, which stays a fact about the planilla no filter can talk it out of.

Every change to a (customer, circuit) pair’s collected total triggers a full FIFO redistribution of that circuit: oldest due_date first, each document capped at its invoice_balance, partial fill on the last document reached. The redistribution is idempotent and discards manual Imputar edits, so the result depends only on the current collected amount, the documents’ due dates and their outstanding balances. FIFO ordering is byte-identical to collection.receipt._compute_line_ids, so the receipt’s Applications page and its stored document_numbers summary always name documents in the order the money followed.

The board writes into the existing collection.* models only — it stores the validated proposal and never touches Odoo accounting. Customers can be closed individually (a note is required when a circuit still shows a difference); a closed customer freezes and later redistributions skip it. The close state, note, closer and date change only through the board’s close and reopen actions.

The board and its export belong to Collections Users; a plain internal user can neither read nor change them. Amounts typed on the board are read in the user’s own number format: a thousands separator counts only in groups of three digits, and anything ambiguous is refused rather than guessed, so 500.50 typed on an es_AR board never becomes fifty thousand. A retention certificate is tax the customer withheld on their own documents, so it moves only between that customer’s invoice and quotation circuits, never to the pool or to another customer. Two operators can work the same board: every move locks the vouchers and customers it touches, so a concurrent move is retried on fresh data and refused normally instead of over-assigning a voucher. Money split across customers counts for each of them by its share, so Close and the Planilla agree with the board. When the board still holds money in the pool or customers that are not closed, the sheet’s Close button asks a Collections Manager to confirm, listing the pending pool money, its voucher count and the open customers; it warns and never blocks.

The proposal can leave Odoo as a spreadsheet: the header button Exportar Asignaciones (visible on a sheet in proposal and on the closed sheet afterwards) downloads a two-sheet workbook built in the same visual language as the Planilla de Cobranza. Resumen carries one row per (customer, circuit) — collected, applied, on account, total debt, pending, plus the per-circuit status.

Clientes stacks one section per customer, and inside it one section per circuit — which is to say, per collection.receipt. Each circuit shows the documents it imputed in the FIFO order the money followed, then the vouchers that funded it as one Comprobantes — <tipo> block per payment method, each reshaped to the fields that method actually has: a cheque prints bank, type and both dates, a transfer prints its two accounts, a retention its kind. Then any cash a remittance replaced, then the circuit’s balance — cobrado, aplicado, a cuenta — read straight off the subtotals above it. The circuit balances add into a customer total, the customer totals into a sheet total, and the sheet closes with the money no receipt claimed and a reconciliation against the sheet’s own voucher total. A voucher split across circuits is highlighted and only ever subtotalled by the share it put into that receipt.

The colouring is a deliberate ladder rather than decoration: the customer, circuit and block headings step dark to light through one blue family so the nesting is legible at a glance, subtotals stay quiet and are closed by a rule instead of a louder fill, and the four total levels — block subtotal, circuit balance, customer total, sheet total — are ranked by weight and size. Data rows carry a faint stripe to help the eye cross nine columns. The only warm accent is a split voucher, and the reconciliation’s Diferencia row turns green or red so a sheet that does not close says so.

Both sheets are built from the board’s own helpers, so the workbook can never disagree with the screen. Text is always written as text: a customer name or a note that starts with = never turns into a formula.
